# R002A Dependency and Licensing Audit for Wasteland Forge

## Executive findings

This audit supports the architecture you suspected, but with a stronger constraint than the original brief implied: **Wasteland Forge should be built as an orchestrator and capability layer that stands on top of the mature New Vegas ecosystem, not as a replacement for it.** The evidence is strongest for xNVSE, JIP LN, JohnnyGuitar, ShowOff, UIO, MCM, xEdit, MO2 and GECK Extender. These projects already provide the runtime hooks, UI conventions, editor extensions, conflict inspection, and mod-management surfaces that Forge would otherwise spend years re-creating. citeturn37view0turn39view0turn18view0turn33view1turn38view0turn14search11turn16view1turn25view0

Legally, the landscape is mixed rather than uniform. Some projects expose clear open-source licences in public repositories, such as JohnnyGuitar under LGPL-2.1-only, UIO under MIT, xEdit under MPL-2.0, MO2 under GPL-3.0, and JIP PP LN under GPL-3.0. Others have a split posture where a GitHub repo advertises an open-source licence, but the Nexus page for the distributed archive still says “do not upload” or “do not modify” the file. JIP LN, UIO, and GECK Extender all show this tension in the sources reviewed. xNVSE is even clearer at the policy level: the team explicitly says you are free to make mods that depend on xNVSE, but you are **not** allowed to repackage xNVSE with your mod. MCM likewise says it must not be redistributed with mods that use it. citeturn35search0turn1search12turn7view1turn8view6turn23view1turn39view0turn39view3turn25view0turn37view0turn34view1turn38view0

Technically, the ecosystem is friendly to a capability-driven design. In-game, xNVSE exposes version and plugin checks such as `GetNVSEVersion`, `IsPluginInstalled`, and `GetPluginVersion`, with documented plugin names for JIP LN, JohnnyGuitar, ShowOff, MCM and UIO. xNVSE’s own development documentation also distinguishes runtime and GECK build configurations, which is exactly the split a Forge runtime-adapter layer would need. Outside the game, xEdit exposes a script API and automation-friendly command-line modes, while MO2 has documented Python and C++ plugin APIs. citeturn12search7turn29search1turn12search1turn31search0turn36view0turn15view3turn14search5turn16view0turn16view1

Socially, the evidence argues very strongly against replacement. xNVSE, JIP LN, JohnnyGuitar, ShowOff, UIO, MCM and MCM Extender all have substantial “Mods using this mod” counts on Nexus, and MCM Extender itself now assumes a layered dependency stack including xNVSE, JohnnyGuitar, ShowOff and UIO. JIP PP LN is the exception: it is promising, publicly licensed, and explicitly a continuation of JIP LN, but its adoption is still tiny in comparison, and MCM Extender’s 2026 changelog shows active compatibility checks around it and JohnnyGuitar’s JIP fixes. That makes JIP PP LN a good optional compatibility target, but not a safe hard baseline for Forge today. citeturn34view0turn33view0turn34view2turn33view2turn33view1turn34view1turn34view3turn33view3turn40view0

The practical conclusion is that **ADR-002A should land on “depend and integrate; do not replace; bundle only in narrowly defined cases.”** Forge should treat xNVSE as the baseline runtime substrate, treat JIP LN/JohnnyGuitar/ShowOff/UIO/MCM as capability modules, treat MCM Extender as a particularly attractive generation target for menus, and treat xEdit, MO2, GECK and GECK Extender as external tools to automate and extend rather than absorb. citeturn37view0turn39view0turn18view0turn33view1turn40view0turn14search1turn16view1turn25view0

## How to read this audit

The table below separates three different questions that are easy to conflate.

“**Can use**” means Forge can safely depend on the project being present, target its APIs, or generate outputs for it. “**Can bundle legally**” is a conservative assessment of whether redistribution appears supportable from the public sources reviewed here. Where public repository licences and Nexus archive permissions diverge, I treat that as **conditional**, not cleanly yes. “**WFG policy**” is the social and platform-governance recommendation: even if redistribution may be legally possible, that does not mean bundling is a good ecosystem move. This is an informational audit, not formal legal advice. citeturn27search3turn37view0turn39view0turn39view3turn25view0

## Legal and redistribution findings

The single most important legal pattern is that **“public repository” and “safe to rehost official binary” are not the same thing in this ecosystem**. JIP LN shows a GitHub GPL-3.0 repo, yet its Nexus page says you are not allowed to upload the file to other sites and must get permission before modifying the files. UIO shows the same kind of split: a GitHub MIT repo, but a Nexus page that says no upload and no modification without permission. GECK Extender also follows the same pattern with a GPL-tagged repo and a restrictive Nexus archive page. From a Wasteland Forge governance perspective, that means the safe default is: **do not redistribute someone else’s official Nexus archive just because a source repo exists somewhere.** If redistribution ever becomes necessary, do it only from clearly licensed source with full licence compliance, or after explicit maintainer permission. citeturn39view0turn1search12turn39view3turn7view6turn25view0

Two projects are much cleaner at the policy level. xNVSE explicitly says that mods may depend on it, including mods that opt into donation points, but that authors are **not allowed to repackage xNVSE** with their mod. MCM says essentially the same thing in practice: mods using MCM should still work without making it a master, but if a mod requires MCM, the author should provide a link rather than redistribute MCM. Those two statements are unusually direct and are strong evidence for a Forge packaging rule that links to official downloads rather than mirroring them. citeturn37view0turn34view1

The cleanest redistribution cases are the pure developer tools and the explicitly licensed code-centric projects. MO2’s repository declares GPL-3.0. xEdit’s repository declares MPL-2.0. JohnnyGuitar declares LGPL-2.1-only. JIP PP LN declares GPL-3.0 on both GitHub and Nexus. Those are the best candidates for lawful redistribution **in theory**, subject to normal compliance obligations. Even so, the social recommendation is still to avoid bundling them into Forge’s main distribution unless there is a very strong reason, because legal permission does not eliminate maintenance burden, update lag, user confusion, or the feeling that Forge is trying to become a shadow package manager. citeturn8view6turn7view1turn35search0turn23view0turn23view1

MCM and MCM Extender sit on the opposite end. In the public sources reviewed here, both are governed mainly by restrictive Nexus permissions rather than a clearly surfaced permissive repository licence. MCM says no upload, no modification without permission, and no redistribution with mods using it. MCM Extender says core assets cannot be included or modified. That makes the governance answer straightforward: Forge may target them, generate content for them, and detect or guide installation around them, but it should **not** ship them in-core. citeturn38view0turn39view2turn40view0

## Technical integration and capability detection

For runtime dependencies, the best design is a **capability registry** instead of a monolithic “required mod list.” xNVSE exposes `GetNVSEVersion`, and its plugin ecosystem can be queried with `IsPluginInstalled` and `GetPluginVersion`. The GECK Wiki documents stable plugin names for MCM (`"MCM Extensions"`), JohnnyGuitar (`"JohnnyGuitarNVSE"`), ShowOff (`"ShowOffNVSE Plugin"`), UIO (`"UI Organizer Plugin"`), and JIP LN (`"JIP NVSE Plugin"`). For JIP PP LN, the current documentation explicitly says to detect availability with `GetPluginVersion "JIP PP LN"`. That is exactly enough to let Forge-generated runtime code turn features on and off without hard-failing the whole framework. citeturn12search7turn29search1turn12search1turn31search0turn32view0turn32view1

The outlier is GECK Extender. The same plugin-name lists on GECK Wiki explicitly say GECK Extender cannot be checked with `IsPluginInstalled` and `GetPluginVersion`. In practice, that means Forge should treat GECK Extender as an **editor-side dependency detected externally** by installation or file presence, not as an in-game capability probe. The installation notes also matter: GECK Extender requires replacing `GECK.exe` with the provided one and installing the extender plugin, which is another reason not to casually bundle it into Forge. citeturn29search0turn29search1turn33view4

xNVSE’s own development documentation is especially relevant to Wasteland Forge’s architecture. It distinguishes ordinary runtime builds from “GECK” build configurations, and explains that `#if RUNTIME` and `#if EDITOR` gate code for game versus editor use. In other words, the ecosystem itself already encodes the split between runtime adapters and editor adapters. If Forge ever grows native DLL components, the technical model is already there: a small runtime layer for in-game capability hooks, and a separate editor-side layer for authoring automation. citeturn36view0

MCM should be treated as **optional presentation**, not foundational runtime state. Its own page says mods using MCM do not need to make it a master; the mod will still work if the user does not have MCM installed, though the menu obviously will not appear. That is a very useful constraint for Forge: generated systems should keep gameplay logic out of MCM and confine MCM integration to discoverability, settings, and diagnostics surfaces. citeturn34view1

MCM Extender is technically one of the most attractive targets for Forge output even though it is not a safe bundling candidate. Its documentation says it introduces a JSON-based framework for feature-rich, script-free MCMs, automatic INI handling, requirements checks, and even menus for mods that no longer need to be an ESM or ESP just to expose configuration. It also says it does not include or modify the original MCM files and remains backwards compatible. That is almost tailor-made for a “Registry Generator” or “Dialogue/Settings Compiler” in Forge. citeturn40view0

Outside the game, the tool integration story is equally strong. xEdit’s official documentation presents it as an advanced module editor and conflict detector, documents automation flags like `-quickautoclean`, `-autoload`, and `-autoexit`, and exposes a script API, with scripts launched via `-script:` and backed by the `xEditAPI.pas` declarations. MO2’s official documentation says plugins can be written in either C++ or Python, and shows concrete installation and authoring patterns for Python plugins placed in MO2’s `plugins` directory. Those are not vague compatibility hints; they are explicit extension surfaces. citeturn14search1turn14search11turn14search5turn15view3turn16view1turn16view0

## Social and ecosystem implications

The ecosystem is not just mature; it is **stratified around shared expectations**. On Nexus alone, xNVSE reports 1,519 dependent mods, JIP LN 3,485, JohnnyGuitar 1,776, ShowOff 844, UIO 227, MCM 225, and MCM Extender 132. Those counts are an imperfect proxy, but they clearly show that depending on these runtime layers is normal community practice rather than an architectural gamble. By contrast, JIP PP LN reports only 12 dependent mods in the reviewed snapshot, which makes it materially less safe as a hard baseline for a general-purpose framework today. citeturn34view0turn33view0turn34view2turn33view2turn33view1turn34view1turn34view3turn33view3

You can also see the ecosystem’s dependency convergence in MCM Extender’s own 2026 requirements and changelog. It now requires xNVSE 6.4.8+, JohnnyGuitar 5.28+, and ShowOff 1.84+, includes native requirements checking, and added warnings/checks around JIP PP LN because it was “currently incompatible with JohnnyGuitar’s JIP Fixes.” In other words, even the newer framework-style mods are being built as layered extensions on top of shared community standards, not as attempts to supersede them. Forge should follow that pattern. citeturn40view0

There is also explicit anti-replacement social signalling in the source material. ShowOff’s README says it does **not** replace JIP LN, JohnnyGuitar, or SUP and is meant to be used alongside them. xNVSE tells downstream mod authors to link users to the main xNVSE release rather than package it with their own mods. MCM says the same for itself. These are not mere licence technicalities; they are ecosystem norms about custody, updates, and author ownership. A Forge that quietly rehosts or subsumes these tools would be fighting the grain of the community that it wants to serve. citeturn18view0turn10search1turn34view1

That same logic applies even more strongly to xEdit and MO2. xEdit is already the conflict detector and record-inspection workhorse. MO2 already has plugin APIs and sits at the centre of profile-driven mod management. Rebuilding either would create a second centre of gravity without solving a proven ecosystem gap. The stronger move is to make Forge feel like a first-class companion to both. citeturn14search1turn14search11turn16view1turn16view0

## ADR-002A recommendation

**Recommended decision:** Wasteland Forge should adopt a **capability-driven, hybrid orchestration model**.

Forge should **build** the things the ecosystem does not already provide well: project scaffolding, registry generation, asset validation, dialogue/menu generation, release pipelines, documentation generation, compatibility reports, and save/runtime diagnostics that sit above existing tools. It should **integrate** with xEdit, MO2, GECK and GECK Extender, because those tools already own record editing, mod deployment, and editor augmentation. It should **extend** the runtime stack by detecting xNVSE, JIP LN, JohnnyGuitar, ShowOff, UIO, MCM and optionally JIP PP LN, then turning features on accordingly. And it should **avoid** replacing xNVSE, xEdit, MO2, GECK, MCM or UIO. citeturn36view0turn15view3turn16view0turn16view1turn33view1turn34view1turn40view0

A practical governance rule follows directly from this audit:

**WFG-001**  
**No proprietary or redistribution-unclear dependency may become a hard requirement of Wasteland Forge core, and no third-party runtime binary should be rehosted by default.**  
Dependencies should be acquired from official sources whenever possible; runtime features should be capability-detected; and external-tool integrations should prefer public APIs, plugin systems, scripts, or generated configuration over replacement. This rule is directly aligned with the published dependency policies of xNVSE and MCM and with the mixed GitHub/Nexus licensing signals found elsewhere in the ecosystem. citeturn37view0turn34view1turn39view0turn39view3turn25view0

In concrete terms, the platform shape that best survives this audit is:

- **Core CLI / generators / validators / docs / release pipeline** built and owned by Forge. citeturn15view3turn16view0turn40view0
- **Runtime adapter layer** built by Forge, but thin, capability-driven, and xNVSE-first. citeturn12search7turn29search1turn36view0
- **xEdit integration** for record analysis, cleaning, conflict visibility, and scripted transforms. citeturn14search1turn14search11turn15view3
- **MO2 integration** for profile-aware developer workflows rather than a new mod manager. citeturn16view0turn16view1
- **MCM and MCM Extender output generation** for menus, but only as optional presentation layers. citeturn34view1turn40view0

## Open questions and limitations

A few parts of the licensing picture remain intentionally conservative.

I did **not** find a single uniform publishing model across the ecosystem. In several cases, the source repository communicates one thing and the Nexus archive page communicates another. Where that happened, I defaulted to the safer platform-governance answer: do not mirror the archive, and do not make bundling central to Forge’s design. That caution applies especially to JIP LN, UIO, and GECK Extender. citeturn39view0turn39view3turn25view0

For xNVSE, MCM, and MCM Extender, the reviewed sources were enough to answer the practical dependency question, but not always enough to produce a clean SPDX-style statement for every distributed artefact. That does not block Forge architecture, because the safer approach is already clear: depend, detect, integrate, and link to official pages rather than redistribute. citeturn37view0turn38view0turn40view0

JIP PP LN remains strategically interesting but operationally unsettled. It is publicly licensed and technically capable, but it is still a low-adoption fork relative to JIP LN, and current ecosystem tooling is explicitly checking around compatibility issues involving it. Forge should support it as an optional capability profile, not a foundation, until adoption and compatibility stabilise. citeturn23view0turn23view1turn33view3turn40view0
